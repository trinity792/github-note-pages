# Week 4 - How Was the World Datafied?
## Lecture 8 – 9/14 Population and States
- Top down power. States impose power upon people. Describing the political powers within the social life of the population
- Why European States start collecting population data:
	- "Biopower" a form of state practice => power and legitimacy of states relies on cultivating the population like a resources rather than a monopoly on violence
- Rulers count population for taxes
- Today: State power <=> sovereignty <=> biopower
- State and statistics => ways for states to exercise powers
	- political arithmetick
	- Statistik (descriptive)
### Political Arithmetic
Case Study: Graunt's "Table of Casualities"
- Moves from the individual to an abstract aggregate
	- strips individuality; people as stats not individuals
### Statistik
- Administrative grid is the process how people get made into data(?)
- Simplifies the complexity of reality in the service of state management and state power
- Shared procedures get imposed from above to establish a standard. States responsible for training on how to use the categories and set the standard. They make reality fix into the prescribed boxes. **Ties back to "Performativity" HCE tool**
**Case study: The US Census**
- Who counts and who matters determined

### Takeaways
- See how statistics, the state, and populations have made one another
	- Through the state’s interest in governing territory and increasing power
- States “see” their populations via simplifying abstractions
	- Through creating an “administrative grid”
	- Abstractions reflect assumptions, interests, and norms of people who hold power 
	- Performative: bring the phenomena they describe into being
- Statistics lets society see itself in new ways
	- View of the sovereign, view from above
	- Abstract numbers, tables, and graphs
	- Social regularities, constituting “society” or “social reality”
	- Interests in “norming” populations, assessing difference
