# Week 10 - Tech Workplace
## L25 The Tech Workplace, Part 1
### Takeaways
- The culture of Silicon Valley isn’t just natural
	- It “fits” certain people or groups, and not others
	- It naturalizes particular, often familiar forms of social power
- While there are ways to change culture, change is hard, and will be resisted
- The view from below is critical
	- People who don’t “fit” a system may be best positioned to understand it
	- Robust knowledge often comes from below/outside
- Change may take more than individual agency
	- This means looking for spaces of agency with others
## L26 The Tech Workplace, Part 2
### Takeaways
- Diversity has long been an issue in the tech workplace
	- Tech culture and power structures have been highly resilient to change
- Positionality matters
	- Intersectional lenses help us see how experiences in tech are shaped by multiple, intersecting factors like race, gender, and immigration status
	- Workers from Latinx, African American, and Indian backgrounds often face specific, layered barriers that numbers alone can’t capture
- Change may take more than individual agency
	- Individuals have tried to create spaces for agency -- but doing it on the inside is hard
	- Looking for spaces of agency with others
		- Workers have built their own networks of support
		- Collective action and labor organizing have looked like an answer to some
