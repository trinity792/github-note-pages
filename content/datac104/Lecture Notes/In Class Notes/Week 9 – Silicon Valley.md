# Week 9 Silicon Valley
## L22 Making Silicon Valley I
### Takeaways
- There’s a standard story of Silicon Valley
	- About: Materials and technologies
	- Actors: Technologists and entrepreneurs
	- Underneath: Networks, the military, and capital
- This story “naturalizes” how the Valley works
	- Makes it seem natural and inevitable, which reinforces the cycle
	- Allows those who rise to success in one wave to ride the next
- For next time: Silicon Valley has a bigger, broader history
	- It’s not just “natural,” to be taken for granted
	- How its history gets told matters (think about what you now know about Stanford)
	- And especially: who gets seen as the agents of history (progress)

### Keys to our standard SV story
- Regional ecosystem of tech activity (network effect)
- Corporations with “free movement” of technical labor
- Military-industrial complex
- West Coast imagination, ambitious engineers who embody it
- **A narrative of up and to the right**
- **Projecting (and shaping) the future is the game**
- **Feedback, reinvestment, and cumulation of rewards over time**
- **Entrepreneurship and venture (risk) capital**
## L23 Making Silicon Valley II
### Takeaways
- There’s a standard story of Silicon Valley
	- We’ve now added (venture) capitalism to the story of materials and engineers
- This story “naturalizes” how the Valley works -- makes it seem natural and inevitable
	- Which reinforces the cycle
	- And allows those who rise to success in one wave to ride the next
- This played out through the dot com bubble and to the rise of the big internet platforms
- Platforms became a key infrastructure of the datafied world, making data into a business model and a kind of capital to be accumulated.
### Tools
- Narratives
- Identity/positionality
- Performativity
- Power
### Key Terms

- Disruptive innovation

- “Move fast and break things”
- Dot-com bubble
- Platforms
