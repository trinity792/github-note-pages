## Lecture 19 Data Futures
- Futures and Narratives
- **Futuring**
- *Narratives of the datafied future*
- Narratives around futures have an aspect of performativity.
- Narratives inform how the narrator sees he present 
- Narratives have structure, they structure our thinking, seeing , etc (they do work)
- Narratives work with time
- Data & Narratives
- "Narratives are pervasive and do work"
### Takeaways
- Examine how futuring works
	- Think about why we do it, and how
- Recognize and analyze narratives
	- Look for them everywhere
	- Ask where they come from
	- Ask what work they do
	- Recognize how certain narratives are templates
- Denaturalize narratives that you’re handed
	- Look for whose narratives these are
		- How they’re located in context
		- How they universalize a particular future or vision of what’s human
	- And whose narratives they’re not

## Lecture 20 AI Futures
- Narrative of transhumanism
- 
### Takeaways
- More than just a technical system, “AI” is a future that has been framed by several powerful narrative templates.
	- Looking at these narrative templates, we can see how AI has served as a mirror for the human condition, social inequality, and history
- The contemporary AI industry, AI research, and public discourse about AI have been powerfully shaped by science fiction and especially by transhumanist narrative templates about merging with machines, longevity, immortality, transcendence, apocalypse, and space travel. 
	- These narratives are technologically determinist, can be interpreted with respect to gender, class, labor and more. 
	- Looking at what’s left out of these narratives can help us think about what they do and who they serve, as well as look to alternatives.

## Lecture 21 Automation and Labor
### Takeaways
- Although there are multiple strategies for firms trying to capture the value of AI as profits, ultimately they rely on the expectation that it will dramatically increase labor productivity and thereby increase economic growth.
    
- This is the context for narratives about the expected impact of AI on the future of work, especially those that foreground the prospect of mass unemployment, which are shaping public perception of AI and the kinds of action that ought to be take around it.
    
- Automation is not just a kind of machine, but a sociotechnical process and relationship between workers, capitalists, and machines.
    
- We can better understand the dynamics of automation by viewing it as a kind of capital that structures this social relationship, starting with the historical precedents set by the industrial revolution.
