Clayton’s central argument is that **statistical thinking and eugenicist thinking are deeply and structurally intertwined**. Modern statistics was not developed as a neutral, value-free mathematical toolkit that was later co-opted by bad actors; rather, foundational techniques—including correlation, regression, chi-squared tests, \(p\)-values, and null-hypothesis significance testing (NHST)—were **explicitly engineered by Francis Galton, Karl Pearson, and Ronald Fisher to quantify racial differences, prove genetic hierarchies, and justify eugenic social policies**.

Furthermore, Clayton argues that these three pioneers deliberately constructed a **"myth of objectivity"** and an image of the statistician as an impartial "numerical referee" to shield their racist and elitist social agendas behind a veneer of unassailable mathematical authority. Consequently, modern efforts to rename awards or remove eugenicists' names from university buildings are merely surface-level symbolic actions; true reform requires a fundamental **reckoning with the discipline's origins, abandoning the illusion of perfect objectivity, and democratizing the data analysis process**.
