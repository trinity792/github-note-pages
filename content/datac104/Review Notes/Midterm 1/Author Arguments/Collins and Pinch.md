Collins and Pinch’s central claim is that public distrust in science during crises stems from a flawed relationship between scientific institutions and lay experts. Rather than acknowledging the uncertain, open-ended nature of research, scientists and governments project a false "public face of certainty" (p. 117) and paternalistic control, while ignoring valuable lay craft knowledge. When overconfident expert predictions inevitably fail, the public is driven into **"flip-flop thinking about science"**—oscillating between viewing science as absolute truth and viewing it as a political conspiracy (p. 124). To prevent this, society must adopt a **"golem science"** perspective that accepts scientific expertise as powerful yet fallible, while respecting and integrating the localized expertise of lay communities (p. 124–125).
