Thomas Kuhn’s central claim is that scientific advancement does not occur through a linear, cumulative accumulation of facts, but through periodic scientific revolutions—where one scientific community abandons an established framework in favor of a new, incompatible one. Kuhn argues that a transition between competing paradigms cannot be forced by purely logical proof or neutral observation; instead, it is a **"conversion experience that cannot be forced"** (p. 151), resulting in an **"increasing shift in the distribution of professional allegiances"** (p. 158). Because early candidates for a paradigm are often crude and solve only a few problems, a decision between paradigms **"must be based less on past achievement than on future promise"** (p. 157) and requires a **"faith that the new paradigm will succeed with the many large problems that confront it"** (p. 158).
- **Definition**: The historian and philosopher of science whose book _The Structure of Scientific Revolutions_ demonstrated that scientific progress does not occur through linear, cumulative addition of facts, but through periods of consensus (_normal science_) interrupted by revolutionary paradigm shifts (`Lecture 6, slides 3–6`; `Kuhn p. 10, 144–158`).
