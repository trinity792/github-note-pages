## Race Classification & Reclassification under Apartheid South Africa
- **Source**: Geoffrey Bowker & Susan Leigh Star, _Sorting Things Out_ (Ch. 6)
- **Summary**: Under the Population Registration Act of 1950, the South African apartheid state mandated that every citizen be registered into rigid, official racial categories (White, Native/African, Coloured, Asian).
- **Additional Context**: Demonstrates the tragic tension between rigid, clear-cut administrative categories and the messy reality of human lives. Bowker and Star detail iconic borderline cases:
    - **Lize Venter**: An abandoned infant whose racial classification was debated by state officials based on physical traits like hair waviness and nose shape.
    - **Johannes Botha**: A white mail carrier whose home was raided by race inspectors because a Coloured man had visited his doorstep to discuss buying a used car.
    - **Ronnie van der Walt**: A famous white boxer suddenly reclassified as Coloured on the eve of a major match.
    - **Colander High School**: A "buffer school" where principals juggled admissions for light-skinned "borderline" children to preserve the school's social standing.
