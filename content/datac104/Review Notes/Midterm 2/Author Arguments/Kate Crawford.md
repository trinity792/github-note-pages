# Kate Crawford
Reading (L24): "Labor," *Atlas of AI*, Chapter 2. Source: [[datac104/Reading Notes/Week 9/Kate Crawford - The Atlas of AI (Chapter 2 Labor)|reading note]].

## Argument
Crawford argues that AI depends on extensive human labor while often concealing that dependence behind an image of autonomous machine intelligence. Warehouse workers, annotators, moderators, and users perform tasks that keep computational systems functioning. The central labor question therefore includes how people are made to work like machines: monitored, measured, and compelled to follow algorithmic schedules and production targets. Automation can reorganize and intensify labor while making it less visible.

She situates algorithmic management within older histories of industrial discipline, including inspection houses, the division of labor, and Taylor's scientific management. Digital systems extend those projects by tracking workers' movements and controlling time at greater resolution. Apparent convenience and efficiency can shift costs, uncertainty, and bodily strain onto workers. Her discussion of labor organizing shows that these arrangements remain contested, with control over working time, dignity, and humane conditions at stake.

## Supporting Examples

- **Amazon fulfillment centers and the rate:** Robots move product racks while workers perform difficult picking and handling tasks under continuous scanning and productivity quotas. Crawford's account of workers organizing against the rate shows that a metric is also a management decision about bodily demands and control over time, rather than a neutral measure of performance.
- **Mechanical Turk and fauxtomation:** Amazon Mechanical Turk coordinates people performing small tasks behind computational services. The reading also describes human workers supporting supposedly autonomous assistants such as x.ai's Amy. These examples illustrate fauxtomation, a term Crawford draws from Astra Taylor: automation claims can disguise the relocation and fragmentation of labor rather than its elimination.
