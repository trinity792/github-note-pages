# Ursula K. Le Guin
Reading (L20): *The Carrier Bag Theory of Fiction*. Source: [[datac104/Reading Notes/Week 8/Ursula K. Le Guin - The Carrier Bag Theory of Fiction|reading note]].

## Argument
Le Guin challenges the **killer story**, the narrative that defines human culture and technological progress through weapons, conquest, conflict, and a solitary hero. Drawing on Elizabeth Fisher's account of containers as early cultural tools, she foregrounds gathering, carrying, storing, and sustaining life. The carrier bag offers an alternative origin story in which technology supports ordinary survival and relationships, broadening who and what counts as central to human history.

She extends this model to fiction: a novel can be a container holding diverse people, objects, failures, tensions, and ongoing processes. Conflict can occur within that container without determining its entire structure or forcing a final heroic resolution. Science fiction can similarly explore everyday relations and ecological coexistence instead of repeatedly staging technological mastery followed by apocalypse. Changing the narrative form changes which experiences become visible and which technological futures can be imagined.

---

## Supporting Examples

- **Gathering wild oats and carrying food home:** The reading emphasizes the importance of gathered plant foods to early subsistence. A container lets someone carry more than their hands or stomach can hold, feed others, and store supplies. This ordinary, sustaining tool challenges the cultural prominence of the spectacular mammoth hunter and his spear.
- **The weapon-centered imagery of *2001: A Space Odyssey*:** Le Guin criticizes the association of human beginnings and technological development with a weapon. This cinematic image exemplifies how the heroic narrative makes violent mastery appear foundational while leaving gathering and care outside the main story. Her carrier bag supplies a different organizing image for both technology and storytelling.
