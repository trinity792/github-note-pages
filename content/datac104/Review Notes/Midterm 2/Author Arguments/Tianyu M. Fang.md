# Tianyu M. Fang
Reading (L23): *Silicon Valley Goes to War*. Source: [[datac104/Reading Notes/Week 9/Tianyu M Fang - Silicon Valley Goes To War|reading note]].

## Argument
Fang argues that Silicon Valley's embrace of defense technology represents a return to its military-funded origins after a period dominated by consumer internet and cyberlibertarian narratives. Cold War procurement and university research helped build the region, complicating the story of independent founders and private markets. In his account, consumer-market saturation, opportunities in defense budgets, dissatisfaction with established contractors, and geopolitical rivalry help explain renewed investment in military systems.

He questions the **democracy paradox** in the industry's justification for this turn: technologies presented as necessary to defend liberal democracy can also enable surveillance, coercion, and the erosion of rights. Appeals to national security and an AI arms race can legitimize commercial expansion and weaken ethical constraints or employee opposition. His argument asks whose interests military innovation serves and whether the means being adopted undermine the democratic values invoked to defend them.

---

## Supporting Examples

- **Y Combinator and Ares Industries:** Fang contrasts an accelerator associated with consumer services such as Airbnb and DoorDash with its backing of a startup building low-cost anti-ship missiles. The case makes the shift toward defense visible in investment choices and in the problems the startup ecosystem treats as desirable to solve.
- **Project Maven and the reversal of ethical constraints:** The reading note recounts Google workers' 2018 opposition to computer-vision work for military drone footage, the contract's lapse, and the adoption of AI restrictions. It then describes later removal of prohibitions on weapons-related AI. Fang uses this trajectory to show changing corporate priorities and the weakening of safeguards previously supported by employee dissent.
