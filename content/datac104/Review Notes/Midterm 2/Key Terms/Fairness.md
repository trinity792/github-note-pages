# Fairness
Lectures: L16. Sources: [[datac104/Lecture Notes/L16 Fairness Automated Decision-Making I|L16: fairness]]; [[datac104/Lecture Notes/L17 Automated Decision Making II - Prediction|L17: prediction]].

## Definition and Significance
Fairness concerns how people ought to be treated and how benefits, burdens, and errors should be distributed. L16 shows that familiar fairness intuitions can be translated into different, incompatible technical criteria.

The course resists equating fairness with accuracy alone. Agency, contestability, history, and the consequences of institutional decisions remain relevant even when predictions are statistically strong or consistently applied.

## Examples

- The COMPAS dispute contrasts equal error rates with equal predictive meaning of a score.
- Ofqual raises the fairness of denying students the opportunity to demonstrate improvement.
