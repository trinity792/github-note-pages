# Anti-discrimination Law
Lectures: L16. Sources: [[datac104/Lecture Notes/L16 Fairness Automated Decision-Making I|L16: fairness]]; [[datac104/Lecture Notes/L18 Automated Decision Making III - Risk|L18: risk]].

## Definition and Significance
Anti-discrimination law supplies a framework for examining unequal treatment or effects involving protected groups. L16 explains how algorithmic fairness research often translates this into comparisons of performance across legally recognized categories.

The lecture treats this framing as useful but incomplete. It can turn justice into an accuracy or liability problem while leaving institutional hierarchy intact. L18 adds that removing explicit group categories can encourage finer-grained surveillance.

---

## Examples

- Gender-based insurance disputes show how anti-discrimination demands can coexist with individualized risk pricing.
- L16's disparate-impact discussion shows that unequal outcomes do not automatically settle a legal evaluation.
