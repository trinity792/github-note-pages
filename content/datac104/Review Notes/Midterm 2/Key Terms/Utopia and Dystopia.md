# Utopia and Dystopia
Lectures: L19. Sources: [[datac104/Lecture Notes/L19 Data Futures|L19: data futures]]; [[datac104/Lecture Notes/L20 AI Futures|L20: AI futures]].

## Definition and Significance
Utopias depict highly desirable societies or futures; dystopias portray oppressive or threatening ones. The lectures treat both as narrative forms that select and transform features of the present.

Their importance exceeds predictive accuracy. They shape what people hope for or fear and can direct investment or political response. L20 also notes that AI doom and abundance narratives can share assumptions about an imminent historical rupture.

---

## Examples

- Smart-city scenarios can make datafied convenience feel desirable.
- The Matrix and Black Mirror render technological dependence or control disturbing.
