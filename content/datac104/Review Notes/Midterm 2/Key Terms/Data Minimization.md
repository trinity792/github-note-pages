# Data Minimization
Lectures: L13. Sources: [[datac104/Lecture Notes/L12 Privacy in the World|L12: privacy in the world]]; [[datac104/Reading Notes/Week 5/The Right to Oblivion - Privacy and the Good Life|Pressly: the right to oblivion]].

## Definition and Significance
Data minimization limits information generation, collection, retention, or circulation to what a purpose requires. L12 asks whether institutions can achieve their aims while collecting less and preventing unnecessary movement across contexts.

The course also raises a deeper question about whether some experiences should become data at all. Pressly's account of oblivion supports attention to limits on the knowable, before a system merely offers settings for information already extracted.

---

## Examples

- A service can ask whether its function requires preserving a conversation rather than assuming that every potentially useful record should be kept.
