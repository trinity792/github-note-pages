# Individual Consent
Lectures: L11. Sources: [[datac104/Lecture Notes/L12 Privacy in the World|L12: privacy in the world]]; [[datac104/Reading Notes/Week 5/Helen Nissenbaum’s Theory of Contextual Integrity|Malkin: contextual integrity]].

## Definition and Significance
Individual consent is a person's authorization for a data practice after receiving information about it. In the inherited privacy model, giving notice and obtaining agreement are central ways of making collection and use appear legitimate.

The course distinguishes formal agreement from meaningful control. Long documents, all-or-nothing access, defaults, and information asymmetries can constrain refusal; moreover, one person's disclosure may affect relatives, communities, or others who never agreed.
