# Panoptic Sort
Lectures: L17. Sources: [[datac104/Lecture Notes/L17 Automated Decision Making II - Prediction|L17: prediction]]; [[datac104/Reading Notes/Week 6/Michel Foucault's Discipline and Punish (Panopticism)|Foucault: panopticism]].

## Definition and Significance
The panoptic sort describes observation and information gathering used to classify people and allocate differentiated treatment. L17 connects automated prediction to an older bureaucratic logic of making people visible, comparable, and governable.

Prediction extends sorting into probable futures inferred from recorded pasts. People can also internalize the categories and modify conduct to remain legible or improve their institutional standing.

---

## Examples

- Education observes, tests, ranks, and channels students into different tracks.
- A risk score can trigger additional surveillance, producing more records to support subsequent classification.
