# Differential Privacy
Lectures: L11. Sources: [[datac104/Review Notes/Week 5 – Privacy|Week 5 privacy review]]; [[datac104/Lecture Notes/L12 Privacy in the World|L12: privacy in the world]].

## Definition and Significance
Differential privacy is the mathematical approach discussed in the notes for bounding how much statistical outputs change when one person's data is included or excluded. Calibrated random noise is used to limit disclosure from released statistics.

The course highlights a trade-off between privacy protection and the usefulness of fine-grained statistics. This approach addresses a defined disclosure problem; it does not by itself resolve all concerns about data collection, group representation, or the purposes of a system.
