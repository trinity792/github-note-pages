# Mechanical Objectivity
Lectures: L14. Sources: [[datac104/Reading Notes/Week 6/Theodore Porter's Objectivity and Authority - How French Engineers Reduced Public Utility to Numbers|Porter: objectivity and authority]]; [[datac104/Review Notes/Week 6 – Making Social Order|Week 6 social-order review]].

## Definition and Significance
Mechanical objectivity is the ideal of reaching decisions through explicit, standardized rules and calculations that suppress personal discretion. It presents an outcome as reproducible and impersonal rather than dependent on the decision-maker's identity.

Porter explains its authority through social conditions: exposed experts may use calculations to defend themselves against suspicion of favoritism. The choice of formula still embodies judgments about which values and outcomes matter.
