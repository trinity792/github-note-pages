# Prediction Paradigm
Lectures: L18. Sources: [[datac104/Lecture Notes/L18 Automated Decision Making III - Risk|L18: risk]]; [[datac104/Reading Notes/Week 9/Mona Sloane - The Business of AI|Sloane: the business of AI]].

## Definition and Significance
The prediction paradigm treats statistical forecasting as a dominant model of rational decision-making, governance, and intelligence. Institutions organize action around probable futures extrapolated from selected past records.

L18 asks what happens to agency when prediction displaces other ways of relating to the future. Sloane adds that predictions become commercial assets whose usefulness depends on the contexts in which they are deployed.

---

## Examples

- Predictive policing and welfare screening turn anticipated outcomes into present interventions.
- Sloane examines businesses selling access to predictive systems and accumulating data for future value.
