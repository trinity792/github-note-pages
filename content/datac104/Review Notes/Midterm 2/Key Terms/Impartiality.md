# Impartiality
Lectures: L14. Sources: [[datac104/Reading Notes/Week 6/Theodore Porter's Objectivity and Authority - How French Engineers Reduced Public Utility to Numbers|Porter: objectivity and authority]].

## Definition and Significance
Impartiality is the ideal of deciding without favoritism or a personal stake in the outcome. Porter's account distinguishes this stance from the expert knowledge required to apply scientific or administrative judgments.

Standardized procedures can make decisions appear disinterested, but uniform application does not settle which criteria are appropriate. The political commitments embedded in a calculation remain relevant even when its operator follows the rules consistently.
