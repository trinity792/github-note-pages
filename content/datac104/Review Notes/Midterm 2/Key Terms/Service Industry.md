# Service Industry
Lectures: L21. Sources: [[datac104/Lecture Notes/L21 Automation and Labor|L21: automation and labor]]; [[datac104/Reading Notes/Week 9/Mona Sloane - The Business of AI|Sloane: the business of AI]].

## Definition and Significance
The service industry includes activities such as healthcare, education, and care that provide services rather than manufactured goods. L21 emphasizes services whose value relies heavily on time, relationships, and human attention.

These features complicate promises that every task can become cheaper through automation. Cutting time or replacing interpersonal work can change the service's quality, while professional status influences whether technology supports or displaces discretion.

## Examples

- Baumol's cost disease concerns rising relative costs where productivity grows slowly.
- Sloane contrasts the effects of AI on recruiters' and dermatologists' professional judgment.
