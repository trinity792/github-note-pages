# Gig Economy
Lectures: L24. Sources: [[datac104/Lecture Notes/L24 Making Silicon Valley III|L24: Silicon Valley III]]; [[datac104/Reading Notes/Week 9/Kate Crawford - The Atlas of AI (Chapter 2 Labor)|Crawford: labor]].

## Definition and Significance
The gig economy organizes short units of paid work through platforms, commonly using independent-contractor relationships. Smartphones connect workers to opportunities and customers, while platform systems coordinate and assess the work.

L24 places this model within longer histories of outsourcing, temporary labor, and shifting risk away from firms. Its expansion also depends on economic conditions, including unemployment and underemployment, rather than technology alone.

---

## Examples

- Ride-hailing divides work into trips.
- Mechanical Turk breaks projects into small online tasks that can support AI datasets and training.
