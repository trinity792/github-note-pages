# Panopticon (Bentham)
Lectures: L13. Sources: [[datac104/Reading Notes/Week 6/Michel Foucault's Discipline and Punish (Panopticism)|Foucault: panopticism]].

## Definition and Significance
Bentham's Panopticon arranges individual cells around a central observation tower. Inmates can see the tower but cannot verify whether an observer is present, creating the possibility of continuous surveillance.

Foucault uses the design to explain disciplinary power: people internalize observation and regulate themselves. The arrangement produces effects without requiring continuous intervention by a particular guard and can be generalized beyond prisons.
