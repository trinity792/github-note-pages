# Contextual Integrity
Lectures: L12. Sources: [[datac104/Reading Notes/Week 5/Helen Nissenbaum’s Theory of Contextual Integrity|Malkin: contextual integrity]]; [[datac104/Lecture Notes/L12 Privacy in the World|L12: privacy in the world]].

## Definition and Significance
Contextual integrity, developed by Helen Nissenbaum and explained by Malkin, defines privacy through appropriate information flows within social contexts. A flow is specified by data type, data subject, sender, recipient, and transmission principle.

Privacy judgments depend on roles, expectations, and the purposes of relationships. Existing norms can also be evaluated against affected interests, ethical values, and contextual goals rather than preserved automatically.

---

## Examples

- Medical disclosure to a doctor does not automatically authorize transfer to an employer.
- Crisis Text Line illustrates a change from a support relationship to commercial customer-service development.
