# Venture Capital
Lectures: L23. Sources: [[datac104/Lecture Notes/L23 Making Silicon Valley II|L23: Silicon Valley II]]; [[datac104/Reading Notes/Week 9/Tom Nicholas The Emergence of Silicon Valley|Nicholas: Silicon Valley]].

## Definition and Significance
Venture capital supplies risk investment to early-stage firms in exchange for ownership and often governance influence. L23 describes staged funding, portfolio losses, and the hope that a few exceptional successes deliver large returns.

This financing shapes company priorities toward scaling and eventual liquidity through an IPO or acquisition. It supplies resources and networks while giving investors leverage over founders and strategy.

## Examples

- Subsidized Uber rides illustrate expansion financed before profitability.
- Nicholas contrasts investment styles emphasizing founders, proprietary technology, and market size.
