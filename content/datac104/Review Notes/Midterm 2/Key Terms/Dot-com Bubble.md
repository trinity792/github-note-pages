# Dot-com Bubble
Lectures: L23. Sources: [[datac104/Lecture Notes/L23 Making Silicon Valley II|L23: Silicon Valley II]]; [[datac104/Lecture Notes/L24 Making Silicon Valley III|L24: Silicon Valley III]].

## Definition and Significance
The dot-com bubble was the speculative boom in internet-company valuations followed by a major collapse around the turn of the century. L23 connects it to expectations that rapid online growth would eventually become profitable.

L24 emphasizes continuity after the crash. Failed firms left infrastructure, users, ideas, and experienced entrepreneurs that became resources for subsequent platform businesses and investment waves.

---

## Examples

- Pets.com and Webvan are examples of failed firms whose concepts resembled later delivery services.
- Google's advertising model illustrates adaptation under pressure to monetize.
