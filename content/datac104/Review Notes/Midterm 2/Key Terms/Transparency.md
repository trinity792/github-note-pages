# Transparency
Lectures: L14. Sources: [[datac104/Reading Notes/Week 6/Theodore Porter's Objectivity and Authority - How French Engineers Reduced Public Utility to Numbers|Porter: objectivity and authority]]; [[datac104/Lecture Notes/L17 Automated Decision Making II - Prediction|L17: prediction]].

## Definition and Significance
Transparency concerns the visibility and intelligibility of procedures, evidence, and assumptions behind decisions. Numerical rules can appear transparent because their steps can be stated and repeated.

The course distinguishes procedural visibility from meaningful understanding or contestability. A specialized formula can remain inaccessible to outsiders, while proprietary predictions may prevent affected people from examining how they were classified.
