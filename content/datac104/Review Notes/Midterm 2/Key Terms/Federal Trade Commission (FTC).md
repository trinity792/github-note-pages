# Federal Trade Commission (FTC)
Lectures: L11. Sources: [[datac104/Review Notes/Week 5 – Privacy|Week 5 privacy review]]; [[datac104/Lecture Notes/L12 Privacy in the World|L12: privacy in the world]].

## Definition and Significance
The FTC is the U.S. regulator discussed in the notes as enforcing aspects of commercial privacy through action against unfair or deceptive practices. The course emphasizes companies' promises in terms of service and privacy policies as an important basis of this oversight.

This approach makes institutional accountability partly depend on what companies said they would do. L12 contrasts enforcing those promises with asking whether the data practice itself respects relationships, dignity, and meaningful choice.

---

## Examples

- A company can face scrutiny for departing from its privacy promises while a troubling practice disclosed in advance may escape that particular objection.
