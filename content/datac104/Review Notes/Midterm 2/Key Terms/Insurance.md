# Insurance
Lectures: L17, L18. Sources: [[datac104/Lecture Notes/L17 Automated Decision Making II - Prediction|L17: prediction]]; [[datac104/Lecture Notes/L18 Automated Decision Making III - Risk|L18: risk]].

## Definition and Significance
Insurance pools contributions to cover losses that affect only some participants. At the same time, insurers can classify people by predicted risk and charge different premiums.

The course contrasts solidarity through pooling with actuarial individualization. More precise pricing can seem fair because people pay for their own risk, while expanding surveillance and making protection harder to afford for disadvantaged groups.

---

## Examples

- Car-insurance sensors monitor driving behavior in exchange for individualized pricing.
- Social insurance distributes risks collectively rather than pricing every person according to a separate prediction.
