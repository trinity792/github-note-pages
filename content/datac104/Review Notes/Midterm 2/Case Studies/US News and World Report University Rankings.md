# US News and World Report University Rankings
Lecture: L14. Sources: [[datac104/Review Notes/Week 6 – Making Social Order|Week 6 review: university rankings]]; [[datac104/Reading Notes/Week 6/Theodore Porter's Objectivity and Authority - How French Engineers Reduced Public Utility to Numbers|Porter reading note: quantification and authority]].

## Case and Significance
The Week 6 review uses U.S. News & World Report university rankings to show how complex judgments about educational quality are translated into an aggregate numerical score and ordinal position. A ranking offers a standardized way to compare institutions, making its formula appear to supply a common basis for decisions that otherwise involve heterogeneous aims and values.

The central concept is **performativity**: rankings help change the institutions they claim to describe. When status and evaluation depend on scores, universities can alter admissions, financial arrangements, and academic priorities to improve their position. Porter's analysis helps explain the authority of impersonal calculations across a society of strangers. It also draws attention to the judgments embedded in choosing what counts and how it is weighted.

## Key Details

- The review explicitly identifies changes to admissions, financial structures, and academic programs as ways institutions respond to ranking criteria.
- The lesson concerns representation as well as incentives: a single hierarchy makes selected measurable dimensions prominent while other forms of educational value become harder to see.
