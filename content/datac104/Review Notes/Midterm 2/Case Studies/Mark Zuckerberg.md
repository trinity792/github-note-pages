# Mark Zuckerberg
Lecture: L23. Sources: [[datac104/Lecture Notes/L23 Making Silicon Valley II|L23: founder culture and disruption]]; [[datac104/Lecture Notes/L24 Making Silicon Valley III|L24: Facebook and data as capital]].

## Case and Significance
L23 invokes Mark Zuckerberg as one example of the Silicon Valley **founder as a social type**. The founder is expected to perform confidence, vision, experimentation, and optimism about building a large future business. Facebook's move fast and break things slogan condenses the course's account of a culture that can celebrate disruption and rapid iteration while deferring questions about consequences or rules.

The case connects individual leadership narratives to the institutions that make them effective. Venture capital selects and funds particular visions of growth, and investors may rely on a familiar image of the successful founder. L24 adds that Facebook exemplifies the conversion of social interaction into accumulated data. Zuckerberg's role in these notes therefore helps explain founder culture and platform business models rather than supplying an independent biographical explanation of technological change.

---

## Key Details

- The slogan illustrates how breaking established arrangements can be treated as evidence of innovation within startup culture.
- Facebook's inclusion among data-centered businesses in L24 shows the broader shift from providing a service to organizing that service as a source of behavioral information and potential value.
