# Peter Thiel and Elon Musk - Sovereignty as a Service
Lecture: L24. Sources: [[datac104/Lecture Notes/L24 Making Silicon Valley III|L24: defense technology and sovereignty as a service]]; [[datac104/Reading Notes/Week 9/Tianyu M Fang - Silicon Valley Goes To War|Fang reading note: the defense pivot]].

## Case and Significance
L24 pairs Peter Thiel and Elon Musk to examine the relationship between private technology firms and state power. Thiel's Palantir illustrates platforms supplying data organization and analysis to governments, businesses, and militaries, with early support from intelligence-linked investment. Musk's SpaceX illustrates a company built partly through public foundations and military demand that becomes essential to major state activities. These cases complicate a narrative of technology firms succeeding independently of government.

The lecture's **sovereignty as a service** argument focuses especially on SpaceX and its satellite infrastructure. When governments rely on privately controlled launch and communications systems, a firm can become an obligatory passage point for exercising public power. State dependence on a platform gives its owners leverage and can make authority and accountability ambiguous. The issue is therefore who controls infrastructure that supports national and military capabilities, beyond who pays for an ordinary commercial service.

## Key Details

- Palantir connects the defense pivot to venture investment and privatized state functions. Fang's account places this development within Silicon Valley's recurring relationship with military demand.
- The lecture uses uncertainty over Ukrainian access to Starlink or Starshield to illustrate the consequences of delegating militarily significant infrastructure to a private provider; it does not resolve all conflicting accounts of control.
