# Wireless and Radio Technologies
Lecture: L22. Sources: [[datac104/Lecture Notes/L22 Making Silicon Valley I|L22: the wireless and aviation layer]]; [[datac104/Reading Notes/Week 9/Tom Nicholas The Emergence of Silicon Valley|Nicholas reading note: regional clustering]].

## Case and Significance
L22 presents wireless telegraphy and radio as an early technological layer of Silicon Valley. Vacuum tubes made it possible to generate, detect, and amplify signals, but devices became consequential through broadcasting installations, amateur operators, manufacturers, suppliers, and users. Ships and aircraft supplied important demand because they required communication without fixed wires.

The case illustrates **sociotechnical systems and regional accumulation**. Electronics developed through overlapping communities and institutions, including naval facilities, aviation networks, local firms, and universities. World War II expanded industrial and communications demand. Silicon Valley's later semiconductor and computing industries therefore grew on existing capabilities and relationships, rather than appearing fully formed with personal computers or individual startup founders.

## Key Details

- The lecture connects ship-to-shore wireless with San Francisco's naval role and U.S. power across the Pacific, linking communications infrastructure to state and military needs.
- Moffett Field and its surrounding aviation suppliers illustrate the convergence of wireless electronics with another regional industry, creating technical communities and markets that reinforced one another.
