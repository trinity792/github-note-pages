# Babbage's Difference Engine
Lecture: L21. Sources: [[datac104/Lecture Notes/L21 Automation and Labor|L21: Prony and Babbage]].

## Case and Significance
What?: connects Charles Babbage's Difference Engine to Gaspard de Prony's organization of mathematical table production. Prony divided the work into a hierarchy: elite mathematicians developed formulas, trained calculators organized intermediate work, and a lower tier performed simple arithmetic. Babbage recognized that repetitive calculations could be embodied in mechanical gears and levers.

Implication: The example shows how **a technical artifact can materialize a social division of labor**. Automation first depends on separating and formalizing activities, making some parts available for mechanization while preserving other roles in planning and design. The machine therefore has an organizational history: it incorporates a particular account of how human work should be decomposed, coordinated, and valued.

---

## Key Details

- The key movement in the lecture is from organized human calculation to mechanical performance of repetitive steps. The social arrangement supplies a model for the machine.
- The case links mental labor to industrial organization, showing that automation concerns knowledge and hierarchy as well as the replacement of physical effort.
