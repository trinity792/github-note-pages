# Elon Musk
Lecture: L23. Sources: [[datac104/Lecture Notes/L23 Making Silicon Valley II|L23: founder culture and PayPal]]; [[datac104/Lecture Notes/L24 Making Silicon Valley III|L24: reinvestment and infrastructure power]].

## Case and Significance
L23 uses Elon Musk alongside other prominent founders to examine the cultural expectation that entrepreneurs confidently project and build a technological future. Such stories can recruit investment, workers, and public belief. The lecture's account of venture capital explains how those performances are embedded in financing arrangements that favor growth, scale, and a large eventual payoff.

Musk also exemplifies **cumulative resources across technological waves**. L23 connects him to PayPal, and L24 describes wealth, experience, and influence from earlier firms becoming resources for later ventures. The larger point is that recurring successful founders do not start each wave from nothing. Their position reflects accumulated capital and networks, while later infrastructure businesses also depend on public institutions and contracts. The separate L24 Thiel/Musk case develops that state-dependence question.

## Key Details

- The founder-archetype discussion makes Musk one instance of a recurring social role, rather than treating personal charisma as a sufficient explanation for the region's development.
- L24's account of resources moving from PayPal into SpaceX links the narrative of entrepreneurial renewal to earlier financial success and government demand.
