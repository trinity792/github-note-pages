# TSA Body Scanners
Lecture: L12. Sources: [[datac104/Lecture Notes/L12 Privacy in the World|L12: TSA body scanners]].

## Case and Significance
The lecture examines post-September 11 airport body scanners that incorporated privacy protections such as blurring facial areas and disabling image storage. These measures addressed identifiability and retention, yet travelers still had to accept scanning or another potentially invasive screening procedure to fly. Screening also relied on classifications and anatomical templates that could mark a body as anomalous.

The case connects **privacy to dignity, power, and positionality**. Operators' male/female selections and normative body templates could expose transgender, nonbinary, and other nonconforming travelers to additional scrutiny and pat-downs. A system can therefore satisfy formal information-privacy requirements while distributing bodily intrusion unequally. Its effects depend on technical design, institutional rules, and human judgments together.

---

## Key Details

- The ability to choose a pat-down instead of a scan does not remove the compulsory character of screening for someone who needs to travel.
- The lecture contrasts the routine inconvenience experienced by travelers whose bodies fit the templates with the heightened scrutiny imposed on those whose bodies do not.
