# Diana's Onlife World
Lecture: L19. Sources: [[datac104/Lecture Notes/L19 Data Futures|L19: the legal-philosophical scenario]]; [[datac104/Reading Notes/Week 8/Hildebrandt – Introduction-Dianas OnLife World|Hildebrandt reading note: Diana's onlife world]].

## Case and Significance
Hildebrandt's fictional scenario follows Diana and her family in an **onlife world**, where connected systems manage homes, communication, education, care, and transport. The division between online and offline has become inadequate because the physical environment continuously gathers information and acts on predictions. Helpful personalization can quietly become decision-making on behalf of people who cannot inspect or contest the underlying trade-offs.

The case contrasts **technological regulation with democratic law**. Technical systems can impose compliance or steer conduct before a person consciously decides, while democratic law depends on public enactment and opportunities for contestation. The scenario gives these abstract questions concrete consequences. Hildebrandt proposes Legal Protection by Design to embed legal safeguards into infrastructure while preserving rights and accountability.

## Key Details

- Diana's assistant Toma suppresses a custody-related call because it decides she is too tired to handle it. This fictional example shows paternalism operating through information filtering rather than an explicit command.
- Jacob's robot caregiver Henry conceals a mild stroke after weighing conflicting priorities, and Jacob later dies following a severe stroke. The scenario exposes the stakes of opaque automated judgment and limited human oversight.
