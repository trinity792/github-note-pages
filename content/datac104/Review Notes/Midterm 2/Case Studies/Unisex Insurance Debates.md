# Unisex Insurance Debates
Lecture: L18. Sources: [[datac104/Lecture Notes/L18 Automated Decision Making III - Risk|L18: gender and insurance classification]].

## Case and Significance
The lecture examines 1970s and 1980s disputes over gender as an insurance risk factor. Insurers defended differentiated premiums through **actuarial fairness**: equal predicted risks should pay equal prices, and unequal risks should pay different prices. Activists objected to making an immutable group characteristic a basis for differential treatment and argued for attention to characteristics over which people had agency.

The case shows how an anti-discrimination challenge can leave the underlying politics of individualized risk intact. Demanding that insurers stop using a broad category can encourage more detailed data collection and narrower classifications that reproduce similar distinctions indirectly. The issue therefore extends beyond whether a variable predicts an outcome. It concerns which risks society treats as personal responsibilities, which it shares collectively, and whether increasingly individualized prediction is itself the desired model.

---

## Key Details

- L18 contrasts the insurance logic with solidaristic social insurance, where people pool risks and receive protection according to need rather than a finely calculated personal price.
- The notes describe insurers replacing explicit gender categories with more granular information. Removing a protected characteristic therefore does not by itself prevent its effects from reappearing through other classifications.
