# Sam Altman
Lecture: L23. Sources: [[datac104/Lecture Notes/L23 Making Silicon Valley II|L23: Y Combinator and founder culture]]; [[datac104/Lecture Notes/L24 Making Silicon Valley III|L24: AI scaling and investment]].

## Case and Significance
L23 presents Sam Altman's relationship to Y Combinator as an example of the networks reproducing Silicon Valley's startup model. The notes identify him as a participant in its first cohort and later its president before his move into AI. An accelerator supplies money and connections while also teaching founders how to identify markets, form teams, pitch growth, and perform the behaviors investors associate with success.

L24 connects Altman's trajectory into OpenAI with the reuse of people, capital, infrastructure, and scaling narratives across successive technology waves. Frontier AI requires substantial investment in data, chips, energy, servers, and expertise. The case illustrates how an apparent new technological beginning is embedded in an existing startup and investment ecosystem, while expectations about future returns help mobilize resources in the present.

---

## Key Details

- Y Combinator serves as both an accelerator and a network, helping institutionalize a reproducible startup playbook rather than merely backing isolated founders.
- The AI-scaling discussion treats graphs of improving performance as narratives that justify investment. Their effects depend on the capital and infrastructure assembled to pursue the projected future.
