# Stanford University
Lecture: L22. Sources: [[datac104/Lecture Notes/L22 Making Silicon Valley I|L22: Stanford, industry, and funding]]; [[datac104/Reading Notes/Week 9/Tom Nicholas The Emergence of Silicon Valley|Nicholas reading note: universities and industrial development]].

## Case and Significance
L22 explains Stanford's rise through deliberately porous boundaries among academic research, companies, government funding, and regional development. Fred Terman built engineering capacity and encouraged connections with local firms. Laboratory access, patent arrangements, consulting, faculty investment, an industrial park, and the Stanford Research Institute linked university activities to commercial and military work.

The case challenges an origin story centered solely on an exceptional university producing inventions. Stanford's development depended on public funding, industrial demand, real estate, and regional networks, and the lecture cautions that its prominence in the standard story can obscure contributions from Berkeley and other institutions. Its institutional history also connects technological development to racial, class, and labor hierarchies, showing how narratives shape who becomes visible as an agent of progress.

## Key Details

- The Varian brothers' microwave work and Hewlett and Packard's connection to Terman illustrate relationships between training, laboratory research, and company formation.
- Postwar federal science and defense funding supported Stanford's expansion, while patents, company use of facilities, and land development made the university an active participant in the regional economy.
