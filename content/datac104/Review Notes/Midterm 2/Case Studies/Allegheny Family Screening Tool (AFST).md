# Allegheny Family Screening Tool (AFST)
Lecture: L17. Sources: [[datac104/Lecture Notes/L17 Automated Decision Making II - Prediction|L17: Allegheny County child welfare]]; [[datac104/Reading Notes/Week 7/Virginia Eubanks' Automating Inequality (Chapter 4)|Eubanks reading note: the Allegheny algorithm]].

## Case and Significance
The Allegheny Family Screening Tool assists child-welfare staff deciding whether to investigate hotline reports. Eubanks's account explains that its data warehouse draws extensively on public-service records and that its prediction targets include re-referral and foster-care placement, which stand in for harm. Families using public benefits, housing, or mental-health services consequently generate records that wealthier families obtaining comparable help privately may not.

The case illustrates **poverty profiling and performative prediction**. Contact with a support system can raise perceived risk, which attracts additional surveillance and produces further records. The institution both assists families and investigates them, creating a double bind in which seeking help may increase suspicion. Human involvement does not automatically solve the problem: workers can defer to scores and reshape their own judgments around the algorithm's categories.

---

## Key Details

- Eubanks contrasts Stephen and Krzysztof: the child with the more extensive family service history receives the higher score despite the observers' greater immediate concern about the younger child. Administrative visibility can outweigh contextual judgment.
- Re-referral and placement record decisions by community members and authorities. Predicting them can reproduce earlier institutional and community bias without directly measuring abuse or neglect.
