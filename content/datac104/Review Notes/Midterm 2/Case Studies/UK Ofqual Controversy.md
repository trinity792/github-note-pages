# UK Ofqual Controversy
Lecture: L17. Sources: [[datac104/Lecture Notes/L17 Automated Decision Making II - Prediction|L17: UK examination grading]].

## Case and Significance
When the COVID-19 pandemic canceled examinations in 2020, the UK examination regulator used an algorithmic substitute incorporating teacher predictions, student rankings, and historical school performance. The system sought to address the anticipated inflation of teacher-assessed grades. Its results provoked protests over downgrading and unequal institutional effects, and the government eventually abandoned the algorithm in favor of teacher predictions.

The case illustrates **foreclosed agency**. Even a hypothetical accurate, group-neutral prediction would deny students the opportunity to perform differently from the historical pattern assigned to them. Past assessments taken under other expectations suddenly became determinants of future opportunities. L17 therefore uses the controversy to move beyond an accuracy-only definition of fairness toward questions about expectations, contestability, and a person's chance to demonstrate improvement.

---

## Key Details

- The lecture reports that 39 percent of A-level students had teacher-predicted grades reduced by at least one grade, while also noting that ordinary examinations had historically reduced predictions at an even greater rate.
- Smaller private-school cohorts tended to benefit, while some high-performing state schools fared worse. The case includes empirical inequality, but its agency problem remains analytically distinct from those distributional effects.
