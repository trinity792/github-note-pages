# The Matrix (1999)
Lecture: L19. Sources: [[datac104/Lecture Notes/L19 Data Futures|L19: The Matrix and futuring]].

## Case and Significance
L19 uses the fictional world of *The Matrix* to introduce **futuring**. In the film's imagined future, people experience ordinary lives inside a computer simulation while AI overlords immobilize their bodies and extract energy. The scenario turns familiar experience into a possible illusion, prompting questions about whether people recognize the systems organizing their lives.

The course treats the film's significance as an intervention in the present. Its value does not depend on accurately forecasting a later technology. By selecting and exaggerating aspects of contemporary control, dependence, and ignorance, it makes certain risks imaginable and emotionally compelling. This demonstrates how narratives frame what audiences notice, fear, accept, or seek to change about datafied life.

---

## Key Details

- The film's contrast between experienced normality and an underlying system of extraction makes hidden infrastructure and dependency central to its dystopia.
- L19 distinguishes prediction from projection: a story can direct attention and action toward a possible future even when it is not offered as a literal forecast.
