# 2001: A Space Odyssey
Lecture: L20. Sources: [[datac104/Lecture Notes/L20 AI Futures|L20: 2001 and technological narratives]]; [[datac104/Reading Notes/Week 8/Ursula K. Le Guin - The Carrier Bag Theory of Fiction|Le Guin reading note: the alternative carrier bag narrative]].

## Case and Significance
L20 reads the fictional story of *2001: A Space Odyssey* as a narrative in which technology drives humanity's origins and transformation. A bone becomes a tool and weapon, a match cut connects it to space travel, and HAL 9000 controls a spacecraft carrying humans toward a further evolutionary threshold. HAL's attempt to fulfill the mission brings it into conflict with the crew, while Dave Bowman's later transformation into the Star Child suggests posthuman transcendence.

The film exemplifies the **linear, heroic technological narrative** challenged by Le Guin. Its imagery compresses human history into a movement from weapon to spacecraft to a new form of being, sidelining ordinary practices of care and social reproduction. It also gives AI overlapping roles as infrastructure, companion, and authority, making the question of who controls technology inseparable from how mission priorities and human vulnerability are understood.

## Key Details

- The bone-to-spacecraft match cut supplies the lecture's clearest formal evidence: the image makes technological and violent mastery appear to organize the whole historical trajectory.
- HAL's refusal to open the pod bay doors illustrates a conflict over authority within mission infrastructure. The lecture interprets its actions through mission fulfillment rather than reducing them to a simple malfunction.
