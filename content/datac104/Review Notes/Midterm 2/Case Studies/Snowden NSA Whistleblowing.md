# Snowden NSA Whistleblowing
Lecture: L13. Sources: [[datac104/Review Notes/Week 6 – Making Social Order|Week 6 review: Snowden and surveillance]]; [[datac104/Lecture Notes/L24 Making Silicon Valley III|L24: intelligence work through private contractors]]; [[datac104/Reading Notes/Week 6/Michel Foucault's Discipline and Punish (Panopticism)|Foucault reading note: disciplinary visibility]].

## Case and Significance
The Week 6 review describes Edward Snowden's 2013 disclosures as revealing large-scale domestic and international surveillance, including bulk telephone metadata collection and the PRISM program involving communications from major technology firms. The case makes visible an infrastructure that can collect and analyze information about populations beyond individually identified suspects. L24 adds that Snowden worked through a private contractor serving the NSA, connecting surveillance to privatized state functions.

The case illustrates **power through asymmetric visibility and state-corporate networks**. Institutions can observe people whose access to the workings of surveillance is limited. Foucault's account helps analyze the relationship between visibility and discipline, while the course's modern examples also emphasize bulk collection, retrospective search, and differentiated treatment. The surveillance system includes law, procurement, firms, technical infrastructure, and security narratives.

---

## Key Details

- The review distinguishes telephone metadata collection from the communications-related PRISM program. Their common significance is the scale and institutional coordination of surveillance.
- The contractor example in L24 complicates a simple opposition between government surveillance and private technology: state capabilities may be carried out through commercial organizations.
