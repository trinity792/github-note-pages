# Facebook Election Turnout
Lecture: L15. Sources: [[datac104/Lecture Notes/L15 Making Decisions|L15: Facebook voting experiment and hypernudges]]; [[datac104/Review Notes/Week 6 – Making Social Order|Week 6 review: the 2010 turnout experiment]].

## Case and Significance
The lecture describes an experiment by Facebook and academic collaborators in which changes to messages shown to users affected whether they went out to vote. The Week 6 review identifies the 2010 election experiment and its social prompt: an I Voted button accompanied by images of friends who had voted. The outcome at issue was turnout, rather than which candidate a person selected.

The case illustrates **second-order decision-making and choice architecture**. Users retain the ability to act, but platform designers organize the environment in which that action occurs. A small interface decision can become politically consequential at scale. L15 connects this influence to hypernudges, where platforms observe behavior and adjust environments, while the turnout experiment itself establishes the significance of changing the prompts people encounter.

---

## Key Details

- The social information supplied by friends' voting activity is part of the intervention, making relationships and interface design jointly relevant to the effect.
- The lecture reports a statistically significant turnout effect. It does not use the case to claim that Facebook determined a particular electoral winner or directly commanded users to vote.
