# Transhumanism
Lecture: L20. Sources: [[datac104/Lecture Notes/L20 AI Futures|L20: transhumanism and technological self-overcoming]].

## Case and Significance
The lecture defines **transhumanism** as the belief that humans can and should use science and technology to direct their evolution and overcome biological limits. It connects early versions to biologists such as Julian Huxley and to projects of longer life, enhanced intelligence, and space travel. Later versions increasingly emphasize computing, AI, the singularity, and private technological enterprise.

The case shows how technological futures express present values and positionality. Promises of transcendence address mortality and meaning in ways that resmble religious narratives of apocalypse and redemption. They also raise political questions about whose life is extended, who maintains the infrastructure, and who controls the transition. L20 links these visions to unequal life chances and elite desires, while cautioning that transhumanist politics have not been uniform.

## Key Details

- Cryonics illustrates dependence on an imagined future capability: preservation is undertaken in the hope that later medicine or mind-uploading could restore a person.
- The lecture's virtual-afterlife example asks who owns and pays for the servers. Supposed escape from biological limits still depends on material infrastructure, labor, and social support.
