# Machinery Question
Lecture: L21. Sources: [[datac104/Lecture Notes/L21 Automation and Labor|L21: machinery, capital, and labor]]; [[datac104/Reading Notes/Week 8/David F. Noble - Social Choice in Machine Design The Case of Automatically Controlled Machine Tools|Noble reading note: social choice in automation]].

## Case and Significance
The **Machinery Question** asks how machinery changes the distribution of work, wealth, and control. L21 presents the central issue as whether technology lightens human toil or becomes a means to discipline workers and extract more output. Its Marxian account treats machinery as capital situated within property relations and the organization of production, rather than as an independent force with a single inevitable social effect.

The case connects technical change to struggles over wages, working time, skills, and worker autonomy. An increase in productive capacity does not by itself determine who receives the gains. Noble's account of machine-tool automation supplies a later example: management favored designs that promised to relocate production knowledge and control away from skilled machinists. The analytical question is therefore who owns, designs, funds, and governs a machine's use.

---

## Key Details

- The lecture reinterprets Luddite machine-breaking as resistance by skilled workers to deployments threatening livelihoods and working conditions, rather than treating it simply as hostility to invention.
- Noble's contrast between record-playback and numerical control shows that competing technologies can organize authority differently even when both automate parts of production.
