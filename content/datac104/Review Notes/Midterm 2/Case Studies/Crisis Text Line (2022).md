# Crisis Text Line (2022)
Lecture: L12. Sources: [[datac104/Lecture Notes/L12 Privacy in the World|L12: Crisis Text Line]]; [[datac104/Reading Notes/Week 5/Helen Nissenbaum’s Theory of Contextual Integrity|Malkin reading note: contextual integrity]].

## Case and Significance
The 2022 Crisis Text Line controversy concerned information from nonprofit crisis-support conversations being repurposed by a for-profit spinoff, Loris.ai, to develop customer-service software. The lecture says users consented before connecting with counselors and that the organizations described the shared data as anonymized. The ethical question nevertheless remained: what happens when information generated through a caring relationship becomes an input into a commercial product?

The case illustrates **contextual integrity and vulnerability**. People seeking support in distress have expectations shaped by the purpose and relationships of crisis counseling. Moving their information into customer-service development changes the context, beneficiaries, and meaning of the exchange. Removing identifiers may address one risk while leaving the changed purpose and unequal distribution of benefit unresolved.

---

## Key Details

- The commercial product was marketed as making customer support more human and empathetic. That apparent continuity in language concealed a substantial shift from helping the data providers to selling services to other organizations.
- L12 uses the case to distinguish formal consent and de-identification from a fuller assessment of relationships, obligations, and appropriate information flows.
