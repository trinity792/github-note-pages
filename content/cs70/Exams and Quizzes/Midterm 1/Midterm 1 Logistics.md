## Exam Format and Topic Attempts
You have **two hours** for two parts: synthesis questions and topic questions. Both are graded **Pass/No Pass**.

| Part | Questions | Coverage | Staff pacing estimate |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Synthesis | 2 | Questions combining topics, requiring selection of relevant skills, or drawing new conclusions from formal definitions | About 20 minutes per question |
| Topics | 12 | Discrete mathematics topics through Stable Matching; same format as the quizzes | About 15 minutes per topic question |

The midterm lets you re-attempt topics you have not yet passed on a quiz. There is no benefit to re-attempting a topic you have already passed.

> [!important] Stable Matching attempts
> The midterm is the **first** opportunity to pass Stable Matching. The **second** opportunity is on the final, according to this logistics announcement.

Do not try to complete every question. Plan which topics you want to re-attempt and reserve enough time for synthesis questions, or attempt synthesis questions early. Synthesis passes count separately toward your grade. For topic questions, staff suggest starting with topics you feel most confident in, then attempting more if time permits. Aim to complete each problem you intend to pass fully.
The exam will include an optional topic checklist at the start to help you plan which questions to answer.

## Clarifications and Grading Instructions
There will be **no clarifications during the exam**. Any errors will be accounted for afterward. Answer according to what you believe is reasonable and move on as best you can.

- Illegible answers will not be considered.
- Only the exam itself will be graded.
- Fill in the entire bubble for true/false and multiple-choice answers.
- Write short and long answers only in the provided boxes.

## Review and Study Resources
Discussion 6 is a midterm review worksheet covering previous midterm questions. All sections will cover the same synthesis-style questions, and attendance will be taken as usual. Time may not permit every topic to be covered, so come with questions.

| Resource | How to use it |
| --- | --- |
| [Synthesis question bank](https://docs.google.com/spreadsheets/d/12jmOKXyCIb_ufRrNsGBsrrYdJr2B05YX3QcZx8k6MW4/edit?usp=sharing) | Previous exam questions of similar style and difficulty to synthesis questions |
| Previous quizzes | Practice the topics you want to pass on the midterm |
| Discussion worksheets and homework | Review the work from the week associated with each topic |
| [Previous exams](https://www.eecs70.org/resources/previous-exams) | Practice both synthesis and topic questions under timed, exam-like conditions |
| Course notes and discussion worksheets | Build your cheat sheet by summarizing actively and doing practice questions |
| Office hours, discussion, and Ed | Ask about unclear concepts or practice problems; use the associated Ed threads for past exams, the synthesis question bank, and past quizzes |

Use about **15 minutes per topic question** and **20 minutes per synthesis question** when timing practice. After a practice exam or quiz, compare your work with the official solutions and understand why any answers were wrong. Add a missing formula to your cheat sheet; revisit the appropriate note for a conceptual misunderstanding. Ask for help when needed, and study the solutions until you can reproduce them.
Staff encourage active learning rather than passive reading. Come to the exam rested: sleep, eat beforehand, spend some time outside, and take time to relax.
