Throughout this problem, $X$ and $Y$ are random variables.

### (a) Variance Definitions
Write the definition of $\operatorname{Var}(X)$ as an expected squared distance from the mean. Then write the equivalent formula using $\mathbb{E}[X^2]$ and $\mathbb{E}[X]$.

### (b) Covariance and Correlation Definitions
Write the definition of $\operatorname{cov}(X,Y)$ using deviations from the means, and the equivalent formula using $\mathbb{E}[XY]$, $\mathbb{E}[X]$, and $\mathbb{E}[Y]$. Then write the formula for $\operatorname{Corr}(X,Y)$ in terms of the covariance and standard deviations. When is this correlation formula defined?

### (c) Compute from Given Moments
Suppose you are given

$$
\mathbb{E}[X]=1,\qquad
\mathbb{E}[Y]=2,\qquad
\mathbb{E}[X^2]=2,\qquad
\mathbb{E}[Y^2]=8,\qquad
\mathbb{E}[XY]=3.
$$

Compute $\operatorname{Var}(X)$, $\operatorname{Var}(Y)$, $\operatorname{cov}(X,Y)$, and $\operatorname{Corr}(X,Y)$. Then compute $\operatorname{Var}(X-Y)$.

### (d) Independence
For the values given above, could $X$ and $Y$ be independent? Explain in one sentence.

Flip two coins independently. The first lands heads with probability $1/2$, and the second lands heads with probability $1/4$. Let $I$ be the indicator that the first coin lands heads, and let $J$ be the indicator that the second coin lands heads.

### (a) Individual Moments and Variances
Compute $\mathbb{E}[I]$, $\mathbb{E}[I^2]$, and $\operatorname{Var}(I)$. Then compute the same three quantities for $J$.

*Hint: An indicator satisfies $I^2=I$.*

### (b) Expectation of the Product
Compute $\mathbb{E}[IJ]$ and $\mathbb{E}[I]\,\mathbb{E}[J]$. What property of $I$ and $J$ guarantees that these are equal?

### (c) Total Heads for Two Coins
Let $S=I+J$ be the total number of heads. Compute $\mathbb{E}[S]$ and $\operatorname{Var}(S)$. Explain why you may add the individual variances.

### (d) Total Heads for $n$ Coins
Now flip $n$ coins independently, each landing heads with probability $p$. Let $I_k$ be the indicator that coin $k$ lands heads, and let

$$
T=\sum_{k=1}^{n}I_k.
$$

Use the indicator random variables to find $\mathbb{E}[T]$ and $\operatorname{Var}(T)$ in terms of $n$ and $p$.

### (a) One Bag, Without Replacement
We have a bag of $5$ red and $5$ blue balls. We take two balls uniformly at random from the bag without replacement. Let $X_1$ and $X_2$ be indicator random variables for the events of the first and second ball being red, respectively. What is $\operatorname{cov}(X_1,X_2)$?

### (b) Transfer Between Two Bags
Now, we have two bags $A$ and $B$, with $5$ red and $5$ blue balls each. Draw a ball uniformly at random from $A$, record its color, and then place it in $B$. Then draw a ball uniformly at random from $B$ and record its color. Let $X_1$ and $X_2$ be indicator random variables for the events of the first and second draws being red, respectively. What is $\operatorname{cov}(X_1,X_2)$?

### (c) Correlations
Compute $\operatorname{Corr}(X_1,X_2)$ in each of the two scenarios above.

### (a) Nonnegative Expectations
Let $X$ be a nonnegative random variable, i.e. $X(\omega)\geq0$ for every $\omega\in\Omega$. Prove that $\mathbb{E}[X]\geq0$.

### (b) Weighted Intersections
Let $n$ be a natural number, $\alpha_1,\ldots,\alpha_n\in\mathbb{R}$, and let $(\Omega,\mathbb{P})$ be a probability space with some events $A_1,\ldots,A_n\subset\Omega$. Prove that

$$
\sum_{i=1}^{n}\sum_{j=1}^{n}\alpha_i\alpha_j\mathbb{P}[A_i\cap A_j]\geq0.
$$

Note that $\alpha_i$ can be less than $0$.

### (c) A Lower Bound on Positivity
Again let $X$ be a nonnegative random variable with $\mathbb{E}[X^2]>0$, and let $I$ be the indicator that $X>0$. Prove that

$$
\mathbb{P}[X>0]\geq\frac{(\mathbb{E}[X])^2}{\mathbb{E}[X^2]}.
$$

It may be useful to prove that $X=XI$, and to consider the random variable $(aX+I)^2$ for various values of $a\in\mathbb{R}$.
