# Ralph H. Turner – “Sponsored and Contest Mobility and the School System”

## Executive Summary

Ralph H. Turner’s seminal essay, “Sponsored and Contest Mobility and the School System,” presents a comparative sociological framework designed to explain structural and cultural differences between American and English systems of education. Turner argues that while sociologists frequently measure rates of upward social mobility, they often overlook the predominant _modes_ of mobility operating within formally open class systems. Central to his thesis is the concept of "organizing folk norms"—simplified, socially accepted conceptions of how social ascent takes place. These folk norms directly shape educational structures, govern student selection, and condition the broader mechanisms of social control required to maintain system stability in stratified societies.

Turner constructs two ideal-typical normative patterns: **contest mobility** (characteristic of the United States) and **sponsored mobility** (characteristic of England). Under contest mobility, elite status is conceptualized as a prize in an open sporting contest that aspirants must win through their own continuous effort, enterprise, perseverance, and strategy. The established elite does not hold the authority to grant or withhold status; candidates must seize credentials presented directly to the public. To preserve social control among the unselected majority, contest systems cultivate a futuristic orientation, instill a universal norm of ambition, and deliberately delay final, irreparable selection as long as possible. Consequently, American schooling prioritizes comprehensive high schools, flexible tracking, delayed college elimination, practical vocational training, and instruction in "social adjustment".

Conversely, sponsored mobility operates through a controlled selection process where the established elite (or its designated agents) chooses recruits early in life based on criteria of presumed merit. Elite status is bestowed like entry into a private club; it cannot be won through uninvited individual enterprise. Social control under sponsorship relies on inducting recruits early into elite culture while simultaneously socializing the unselected masses to accept their relative incompetence, thereby preventing the formation of ambitious lower-class counter-elites. This norm manifests in the English educational tradition through early competitive screening (such as the historical "eleven-plus" examination separating academic grammar schools from secondary modern schools), elite institutional _esprit de corps_, low university dropout rates, and an emphasis on non-practical, classical, or aesthetic education.

## Organizing Folk Norms of Upward Mobility

### The Nature and Function of Organizing Folk Norms

- **Defining Folk Norms versus Mobility Rates**: Turner establishes that within formally open-class societies providing mass education, the organizing folk norm defining the accepted mode of upward mobility is a pivotal determinant of educational structure, often exerting greater influence than the absolute rate or extent of vertical mobility.
- **Societal Simplifications as Institutional Guides**: Folk norms represent simplified collective conceptions of social reality that develop from complex economic, social, and ideological conditions. Although they do not correspond perfectly to objective reality, these implicit norms guide public judgments regarding what is appropriate, fair, and legitimate across specific institutional domains.

### Contest Mobility

- **The Open Competition Model**: Contest mobility conceptualizes social advancement as an open sporting event in which many contestants compete for a limited number of recognized prizes. Victory must be won solely through an aspirant's own initiative, enterprise, hard work, and risk-taking rather than through elite favor.
- **Publicly Visible Credentials**: Under contest mobility, society at large defines and evaluates elite credentials. Consequently, credentials must be highly visible, easily recognized by the untrained masses, and tied to tangible achievements, material possessions, or mass popularity.
- **Delayed Selection and Continuous Opportunity**: Contest norms strictly oppose premature judgments or structural arrangements that grant early advantages to specific contestants. The system delays the final award of status as long as practicable to ensure a fair race and keep all competitors in the running.

### Sponsored Mobility

- **Controlled Elite Selection**: Sponsored mobility rejects open competitive struggle, favoring a controlled selection process managed directly by the established elite or its institutional agents. Individuals do not seize elite status; mobility is a process of sponsored induction into the governing class based on criteria of supposed merit.
- **Elite-Evaluated Credentials**: Credentials under sponsorship serve to identify elite members to one another rather than to the general public. Ideal credentials consist of specialized intellectual, literary, aesthetic, or linguistic skills that require the trained discrimination of the elite to evaluate, thereby protecting the elite from interlopers claiming status on the basis of popular appeal.
- **Early Recruitment and Induction**: Sponsored mobility favors early selection of only the number of candidates needed to fill anticipated elite vacancies. Early selection provides the elite with sufficient time to supervise and thoroughly socialize recruits into elite culture before alternative influences can corrupt their talents.

## Mechanisms of Social Control Under Each Norm

### Social Control in Contest Mobility Systems

#### Managing Disadvantaged Classes Through Delayed Failure

- **Inculcating Universal Ambition**: To ensure loyalty toward a system where most members receive less than a proportional share of goods, contest mobility cultivates a futuristic orientation and instills a universal norm of ambition across all social strata.
- **Forestalling Collective Rebellion**: Society avoids sharp, definitive selection points early in life, delaying an individual's realization of final, irreparable failure until their conventional attitudes and commitment to the system are deeply established. Unambitious individuals are defined as individual deviants rather than members of a cohesive lower-class subculture capable of mounting a collective challenge to the class structure.
- **Deviance as Behavioral rather than Structural**: Because lower-class youth identify with the successful elite and believe they may eventually succeed, deviance typically manifests as an attack on conventional morality (e.g., gang delinquency or bohemian withdrawal) rather than active revolutionary politics aimed at dismantling the class hierarchy.

#### Restraints on Elite Behavior

- **Insecurity of High Status**: Under contest mobility, there is no permanent or "final arrival" because individuals face continuous competitive displacement throughout their adult lives.
- **Avoidance of Mass Penalties**: Because status is not secured from above, elite individuals are constrained primarily by the need to avoid "ganging up" by rival contestants and to maintain a degree of mass esteem. However, special norms of paternalism or altruism are difficult to enforce because there are no standardized mechanisms to evaluate how status was achieved.

### Social Control in Sponsored Mobility Systems

#### Managing Disadvantaged Classes Through Early Selection

- **Teaching Mass Incompetence**: Under sponsored mobility, the primary threat to elite control is the emergence of a strong lower-class movement attempting to seize power. Social control is maintained by selecting elite recruits early and socializing the unselected masses to view themselves as relatively incompetent to manage society.
- **Suppressing Unrealistic Aspirations**: Early selection prevents raising the hopes of large numbers of people who might otherwise become discontented, educated leaders of a class challenging the elite. The masses are encouraged to make "realistic" plans early in life, preserving a sense of mystery around the elite's superior competence.

#### Restraints on Elite Behavior

- **Indoctrination and Paternalism**: Because elite recruits are selected early, freed from competitive strain, and kept under close institutional supervision, they are thoroughly indoctrinated in elite culture. The system fosters strong norms of paternalism toward inferiors, courtesy, altruism, and a sense of collective responsibility for preserving refined cultural standards.
- **Protecting Elite Omniscience**: When a sponsored recruit displays deficiencies, the elite front is maintained by covering up individual shortcomings rather than discarding the recruit, thereby protecting the myth of elite omniscience from public challenge.

## Institutional Impact on Educational Systems

### Student Selection and Secondary School Structure

- **Comprehensive High Schools versus Tracked Systems**: In the United States, contest norms foster the comprehensive high school and junior college, prioritizing the integration of students and avoiding sharp social separation between superior and inferior learners. In England, sponsored norms historically produced rigid institutional differentiation at age 11 via the "eleven-plus" examination, assigning a minority of students to academic grammar schools and the majority to secondary modern schools.
- **Fantasy versus Realistic Aspirations**: American high school students consistently exhibit occupational aspirations that are highly unrealistic relative to actual labor market opportunities, reflecting delayed selection and the norm of ambition. Conversely, English students in secondary modern schools display significantly reduced "fantasy" aspirations, adjusting their goals to match their early track placement.

### Higher Education and University Attrition Patterns

- **Continuous Elimination versus Early Confirmation**: American higher education operates like an extended contest, featuring open state university admissions and junior college "second chances," paired with high undergraduate attrition rates (~39% to 51%) as students face competitive elimination semester by semester.
- **Low Attrition in Sponsored Systems**: In England, where competitive selection is completed prior to university entry, higher education serves to confirm elite status, resulting in extremely high university graduation rates (~82% to 87%) and minimal student dropout.

### Curriculum, Educational Values, and Social Adjustment

- **Vocational Utility versus Elite Culture**: Under contest mobility, formal education is valued primarily for its practical, vocational utility in helping individuals "get ahead" in real-world competition. Under sponsored mobility, education is valued for its capacity to cultivate elite culture, manners, and specialized intellectual or aesthetic pursuits.
- **Training in "Social Adjustment"**: Contest mobility emphasizes instruction in "social adjustment"—preparing students to navigate unstructured, competitive social situations where rules are ambiguous and competitors come from diverse backgrounds. Sponsored mobility focuses on inducting recruits into a homogeneous elite stratum characterized by pre-existing consensus over institutional rules.
